Drawbars

The sound is varied through the manipulation of drawbars. Each drawbar controls the volume of a particular sound component (pushed all the way in is a value of 0, all the way out is max)

  • anal: fader on an audio mixing console

There are 3 groups of drawbars: upper manual, lower manual and pedals.

  • a "manual" is the keyboard part. Hammond organs have 2 manuals.

each individual drawbar generates a relatively pure sound similar to a flute or electronic oscillator, more complex sounds can be created by mixing the drawbars in varying amounts.

  • Because of this, the Hammond organ can be considered a type of additive synthesis.

A very popular setting is 888000000 (i.e., with the 1st, 2nd and 3rd drawbars fully pulled out), and has been identified as the "classic" Jimmy Smith sound.

What each drawbar controls

  • 1 - one octave below fundamental note
  • 2 - 5th, with a more airy tone
  • 3 - fundamental note
  • 4 - one octave above
  • 5 - 5th, with a sound closer to a sine wave
  • 6 - two octaves above
  • 7 - major 3rd
  • 8 - 5th (one octave higher)
  • 9 - three octaves above

Sounds

If we push in all drawbars and turn percussion off, we'll notice that no sounds come out of the instrument. Once we turn on percussion, then we can hear what it sounds like in isolation.

  • note: this is a common drawbar configuration and is popularly used.

In order for percussion to be heard, your keyboard playing must be disconnected (ie. not legato)

  • that is, if you hit one key while another is already depressed, you will not hear the percussion sound.
